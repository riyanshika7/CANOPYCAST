# Avenue Trees for Kolkata

Source: Trees of Kolkata, "Which are good avenue trees"
https://sites.google.com/site/treesofkolkata/articles/which-are-good-avenue-trees
Community field reference for Kolkata, retrieved 2026-08-29. Not a government
document. Species names and stated tradeoffs are reproduced from that article.

## How a roadside species is chosen in Kolkata

Four factors govern the choice. Safety comes first: a sturdy straight trunk,
enough clearance under the crown for traffic, and no thorns at head height.
Native species are preferred because local fauna depend on them. Crown shape
must match the road, columnar for narrow streets and spreading for wide
avenues. Finally, track record matters, so look at how established plantings
of the species have actually fared in the city.

## Roundish crown

Bakul, Mimusops elengi, about 40 feet. A perfect dome-like crown and an
excellent tree for a mid-sized avenue.

Neem, Azadirachta indica, about 40 feet. Light foliage. Surprisingly rare in
Kolkata as an avenue tree despite suiting the conditions.

Amaltas, Cassia fistula, about 35 feet. Yellow flowers, but it goes leafless
in early summer exactly when shade is most needed.

Kadamb, Neolamarckia cadamba, about 60 feet. Fast growing with a dense canopy.
Fallen fruit can be a nuisance on a footpath.

Peepal, Ficus religiosa, about 60 feet. A large tree that needs ample space.

Arjun, Terminalia arjuna, about 60 feet. A massive forest tree, good for
waterside planting.

Spanish Mahogany, Swietenia mahagoni, about 50 feet. Exotic, but excellent
shade for a wide avenue.

## Umbrella-like crown

Jarul, Lagerstroemia speciosa, about 30 feet. Small, ideal for road medians.

Gulmohar, Delonix regia, about 40 feet. Highly susceptible to breaking and
uprooting in strong winds, which matters in a cyclone-exposed city.

Saptaparni or Devil's Tree, Alstonia scholaris, about 45 feet. Dense evergreen,
somewhat susceptible to breaking in a storm.

Indian Tulip Tree, Thespesia populnea, about 35 feet. Smallish, needs good
space.

Wild Almond, Sterculia foetida, about 50 feet. Straight growth, but
unpleasant-smelling flowers.

Tamarind, Tamarindus indica, up to 80 feet. An ideal tree for large avenues,
though slow growing.

Banyan, Ficus benghalensis, about 50 feet. Not good for avenue planting,
because it needs room for aerial roots.

Rain Tree, Albizia saman, about 50 feet. Better avoided, given recent
disease mortality in the city.

## Columnar crown

Putranjiba, Putranjiva roxburghii, about 45 feet. Evergreen and an excellent
roadside tree.

Copper Pod, Peltophorum pterocarpum, about 50 feet. Yellow flowers in April
and May. Branches are susceptible to strong winds.

India Almond, Terminalia catappa, about 50 feet. Shows reddish autumn colour.
Carries a storm uprooting risk.

Semal or Silk Cotton, Bombax ceiba, about 60 feet. Stunning red spring
flowers but scanty shade.

African Tulip, Spathodea campanulata, about 50 feet. Exotic with showy scarlet
flowers. Older trees are susceptible to stem rot.

Honduras Mahogany, Swietenia macrophylla, about 60 feet. Somber appearance,
with minimal branching until some height.
