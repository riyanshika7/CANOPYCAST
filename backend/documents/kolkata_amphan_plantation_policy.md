# Kolkata Plantation Policy After Cyclone Amphan

Source: Snigdhendu Bhattacharya, "A Decade After Aila, Cyclone Amphan Finally
Changes Kolkata's Plantation Policy", The Wire Science, 9 July 2020.

Journalism, not a government document. The decisions below are reported speech
from named officials and academics, not text from a published policy order.

## What changed

Cyclone Amphan hit Kolkata in late May 2020 and uprooted or damaged nearly
15,000 trees. Civic authorities and the West Bengal government decided to stop
planting several species that had been common on Kolkata roadsides.

## Species no longer to be planted on roadsides

Krishnachura, also called gulmohar, Delonix regia. Radhachura, the copperpod,
Peltophorum pterocarpum. Ashoka, Saraca asoca.

All three were planted in large numbers because they grow fast and look good.
Since 2010 they have been the principal victims of every squall to hit the
city, through the nor'westers and squalls of April 2012, August 2016, October
2017, and twice each in 2018 and 2019.

Suchandra Bardhan, professor of architecture at Jadavpur University, put the
distinction this way: krishnachura and radhachura are good options for parks,
but Kolkata roadsides need less brittle trees. Roadsides need narrow canopies
and dense foliage, because there is not enough space for natural growth.

## Species chosen instead

Bardhan named chhatim, Alstonia scholaris; neem, Azadirachta indica; bakul,
Mimusops elengi; and jarul, Lagerstroemia speciosa.

Forest minister Rajib Banerjee said the state decided to plant neem, chhatim,
jarul and deodar, Cedrus deodara, and that about 12,500 three-year-old saplings
and about 4,500 ten-year-old saplings would be planted.

Almond, mango, black plum, sheesham, Dalbergia sissoo, and arjun, Terminalia
arjuna, were also under consideration for roadside plantation.

## Scale

Firhad Hakim, chairman of the KMC board of administrators and the state urban
development minister, said the city needs 30,000 to 40,000 trees to compensate
for the loss, and that an expert committee of environmentalists and municipal
authorities would decide which tree goes where and when.

In the month after the cyclone, city authorities and NGOs replanted more than
500 trees, including a hundred at Rabindra Sarobar and another hundred in the
Maidan.

## Why roadside trees fall

Environmental engineer Somendra Mohan Ghosh listed the causes of avoidable
losses. Branches chopped to protect overhead cables, which disrupts the tree's
balance. Underground cables and water pipes laid so they obstruct roots. Ground
concretised around the roots, after which trees fall in winds as low as
60 km/h.

Ghosh also said he suggested a tree census to the government in 2015 so that a
plantation policy could be drawn from it, and that no census has been done.

## Context

A 2015 report of the National Commission on Urbanisation estimated Kolkata's
green cover at less than 5 percent and open space at less than 1 percent.

Transplantation has mostly been avoided because it costs between Rs 15,000 and
Rs 25,000 per tree, and the city does not have the equipment for it. The
standing compensation rule has been to plant 10 saplings for each tree cut.
